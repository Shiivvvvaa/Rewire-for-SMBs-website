One job, start to end

This is one real build, running end to end.

01

  1. A WhatsApp voice note, a missed call or a web form. Voice is written out, and translated if it needs to be, so every enquiry is text from here on.

  2. The test is yours: a real person, a job you actually take on, not already in the system. Junk, duplicates and people you have turned down go one way and stop there. Everything else goes on.

  3. Everything you already hold on them is pulled in: past jobs, notes, what they last paid.

  4. At the same time, your live stock and rate card are read for what the job would actually cost today.

  5. The run stops here. Nothing reaches your team, but it is written down so you can see what was dropped.

  6. The two lookups come back together and the job is scored on size, urgency and how likely it is to close. A big one pings you. Every one is handed to an owner by your own rules — the trade it needs, the area it is in, who is free.

  7. Score over your threshold — a big job, or a customer you already know — and a message hits your phone straight away, before anything else happens. Under it, nothing is sent and the run carries on.

  8. A priced quote is written from your rate card, in your words, with the right files attached.

  9. It waits for one click. Approve and it goes out as a PDF from your own address. Change anything and your edits go back into the draft and it writes the quote again. This is the only step that needs you.

  10. Nothing happens for a day. If they reply in that time the run stops and the thread lands in your inbox — a person answering always beats a chase.

  11. A day later the agent rings, answers the usual questions and holds a slot in your diary. Picked up and gave an answer, and it goes to booking. Rang out, voicemail or hung up, and it goes round once more.

  12. A text goes out with the quote again and the wait starts over. It goes round as many times as your rules allow, then stops.

  13. The slot is written into your diary and the customer gets a confirmation.

  14. Reminders are scheduled for the day before, so nobody has to remember to send them.

  15. The invoice is raised against the job with a deposit link, and its due date is the test. Paid by it, and the run finishes. Past it, and the job goes round.

  16. A reminder goes out on your terms — how long after, how often, how firmly — then it checks the payment again. Nobody has to ask.

  17. Everything is written back to your systems and lands in Monday morning's report.
